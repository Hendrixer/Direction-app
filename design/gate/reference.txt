For a century, films have been finished by two people in a dark room: a director who knows what it should feel like, and an editor who knows how to make it so. Direction gives the editor's chair to an agent. Gate is the visual language for that room: black for the picture, amber for the director's hand, prism light for the agent's work, and steel for everything you touch.

01

The relationship

How directors and editors actually work

Before drawing a single control, we studied how films get finished. The director–editor partnership has a century of conventions, union rules and hard-won etiquette. Direction should feel familiar to anyone who has sat in that room, and teach the room to anyone who hasn't.

The stages of post, and what Direction turns them into

1

Dailies

Each day's footage is logged. The script supervisor circles the takes the director liked.

In Direction
Agent logs and transcribes. You circle takes; it suggests.

2

Assembly

The editor builds a first cut alone. The director has the right to see it first.

In Direction
Agent assembles from your circles. First look is always yours.

3

Screening

Watch it through, cold. Don't stop, don't fix, just feel it.

In Direction
Full-screen. Press N to mark a moment without pausing.

4

Notes session

The director talks; the editor listens and leaves with a numbered list.

In Direction
Marks become numbered notes. You turn them over.

5

Recut

The editor executes, often trying a note two ways.

In Direction
Agent reads back its plan, cuts, and offers options.

6

Lock

Picture lock. No more changes to the edit; finishing begins.

In Direction
Lock scenes as you go, then the whole picture.

Directors talk in feelings. Editors translate them into frames.

"It drags" is a complete note. Working out that it means trimming three pauses and a reaction shot is the editor's craft.

Notes are written in plain intent, never in editing mechanics. The agent replies with a 
read-back
: its translation, in numbers, before it cuts.

Watch it through before you say anything.

Editors advise screening a cut once without pausing or taking notes, then giving notes after.

The 
Screening
room has no sidebar and no edit controls. Marks drop without stopping playback; you write them up after.

Good editors show you options, not just answers.

Directors circle more than one take, and editors want coverage marked, not only the "golden" take.

For subjective notes, the agent cuts it 
two ways
. You pick A or B, side by side, synced.

The director's cut is protected.

The DGA guarantees features at least ten weeks for a director's cut, and no one may "cut behind" the director during it.

Anything you 
lock
is off-limits to the agent. Nothing changes that you didn't ask for, and every change is shown.

Notes arrive as a numbered list with timecode.

Editors leave a notes session with a list to implement, each item tied to a moment.

The 
notes sheet
is numbered and addressed by timecode, and stays numbered across cuts so "note 4" always means the same thing.

When notes conflict, emotion wins.

Walter Murch ranks what a cut must serve: emotion first, then story, then rhythm, with eye trace and screen geometry last.

Every note has a 
kind
. When two notes pull against each other, the agent resolves them in Murch's order and tells you it did.

Murch's Rule of Six: the agent's tiebreaker, from In the Blink of an Eye

Emotion

51%

Story

23%

Rhythm

10%

7%

4%

In the app, these map to five note kinds a director actually uses: 
Feeling, Story, Pace, Picture, Sound
. The order of the first three is the priority.

02

Principles

Six rules that come from the room

The picture is the brightest thing on screen

Interface lightness stays between 13% and 30%. Nothing glows beside the frame.

Screening hides everything but a glass dock.

Two hands, two colors, two shapes

Amber and round for the director. Prism and diamond for the agent. You never have to read a label to know who did something.

Shape carries the meaning too, for color-blind viewers.

Intent in, mechanics out

You speak in feelings. The agent answers in frames, seconds and decibels, so you can check its reading.

Every note gets a read-back before the cut.

Nothing behind your back

Every change is visible, attributable and reversible. Locked scenes are untouchable.

The changes reel plays only what moved.

One room, one job

Each stage of the work has its own room with one primary action. No screen tries to do everything.

One steel button per room.

Speak the trade

Cuts, takes, circles, marks, turnover, lock. Real post vocabulary, explained the first time it appears.

Never "revision", "AI" or "generate".

05

Marks & materials

Shape says who. Material says what.

Color is never the only signal. Each actor has a mark shape that holds up in grayscale and for color-blind viewers. Each surface is one of three materials with one job.

Pin · director

A mark at a moment. Points down at the frame it means.

Circle · director

A chosen take. Hand-drawn, slightly open, like a pencil on a log.

Diamond · agent

A suggestion or a change. A cut facet of the prism.

Lock · both

Hatched and desaturated scene. Off-limits until you unlock it.

Black

For watching. The picture and the Screening room. No grain, no tint.

Steel

For touching. Brushed gradient with a bright top lip. One steel button per room.

01:12:08

Glass

For floating over the picture: the dock, HUDs, toasts. Blur 18, saturation 1.4.

Elevation is lightness plus a 1 px top highlight, never drop shadows, until something truly floats. Grain sits at 3.5% on the window and 18% on steel, from one cached 192 px tile. It never touches the picture or the export.

Motion

Still while it plays, light when it lands

During playback only the playhead and timecode move. A new cut never swaps in under a playing picture. When the agent hands back a cut, a single prism sweep crosses the scenes it changed: one deliberate moment, then stillness. Click each tile.

Tokens

Three tiers, so meaning stays separate from paint

Primitives are raw values. Semantic tokens say what a color is for. Component tokens bind a part to a semantic token. Designs and code refer only to the last two, so the palette can be retuned without touching a component.

Raw values. Never used directly by a component.
