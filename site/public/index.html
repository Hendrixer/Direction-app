Every film has a director and an editor. You watch the cut and say what you feel: let it breathe, this is too polite. Your editor knows what that means, makes the change, and tells you what it did.

The production

From brief to delivery.

The steps you already know, in the order a film is made. Your editor does the work between them.

  1. 01 Brief

    Say what the film is.

    What it's about, how it should feel, and its shape: as shot, 16:9, 9:16, 1:1 or 4:5. Then choose who's editing.

  2. 02 Dailies

    Circle the takes you want.

    Your editor watches and logs every take: what's in frame, what's said, where focus drifts. You circle the ones worth using.

  3. 03 Cut

    Watch it. Say what you'd say.

    Your editor cuts an assembly from your circled takes. Pin a note to a moment, a range or part of the frame.

  4. 04 Turnover

    Hear your notes read back.

    Before it cuts, your editor tells you what it heard and what it plans to do. Change anything, then let it work.

  5. 05 Finish

    Lock picture. Deliver.

    Lock scenes as they're done, then picture. Direction delivers a master, a web copy and captions.

Does my footage leave my Mac?

Your original files stay where they are and are never changed. To make its decisions, the editor reads what Direction gives it (logs, transcripts and frames) through your own Claude or ChatGPT plan.

Claude Code or Codex?

Either. Each new film asks who's editing and keeps that editor, with your last choice already picked. You can pause or stop the editor at any time.

Do I need an OpenRouter key?

Only to make shots, stills, music and sound effects. It's paid per use, and you set the limits in Settings. Everything else, from logging to delivery, runs without one.

What does it deliver?

A master, a web copy and captions, in SDR Rec. 709. HDR footage is converted to SDR inside the film; your originals stay as they are.
