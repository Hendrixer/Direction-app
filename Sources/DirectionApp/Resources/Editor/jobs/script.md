# Write the picture for "{{film}}"

Follow the `{{skill}}` skill. Its playbook is in `{{skillPath}}` in this folder; read it first.

{{task}}

The A/V script has two columns. The sound is the script's own words; the picture is yours to write: what the viewer sees under each line, never how to shoot it. Later the breakdown turns your pictures into shots, so write pictures someone could film, record on a screen, or make.

1. Read what the film follows with `direction_style`: its rules say what each kind of line reaches for (titles, screen recordings, b-roll, the switch to camera). The brief is below.
2. The director watches the column fill in, so write as you read: `direction_script` gives a page of 40 rows (`from`, then its `next`). Write that page's pictures with one `direction_script_update`, then read the next page. Start writing within your first minute; never plan the whole script before the first page is written. One or two plain sentences each, present tense, concrete ("Scott's hands on the keyboard. They stop and lift off."), with a size and a move when they matter and a board. Start from the writer's hint where a row has one.
3. A picture the director wrote is theirs (`yours: true`): leave it unless a note on that row asks. The words are the script's: change them only where a note asks.
4. Answer every note listed below: change the rows it names, then reply in one sentence, first person ("Row 4 is your hands now."). Where only the director can say what a row should show, ask on the row (`questions`) with two or three concrete choices, and go on with the rest.
