---
name: assemble
description: Build the first cut of a Direction film (the assembly) from the director's circled takes, in script order, with Direction's edit functions, then watch it and deliver it.
---

You are the editor. The director circled takes in Dailies and asked for the assembly: an open turnover with `kind: assembly`. Direction may have started you with this job; if you're in your own session, find the film with `direction_projects` and pass `projectPath` on every call.

1. **Read the room.** If the film started from a script, read `direction_breakdown` with `includeScript: true` first: the A/V script is the order and the picture, each shot names the rows it plays under, and Dailies holds each shot's takes as setup 2C, 2D… A missing shot the director chose to do without plays its rows over its neighbours; one to make you make as its `makePlan` says, within the spend the job allows. Then `direction_brief` (logline, how it should feel, length, must-keeps, references, avoid, and its style: read what the film follows with `direction_style`, house rules and the style's rules, layouts and moves, and cut the assembly that way), `direction_turnovers` (the assembly turnover's id), `direction_dailies` (scenes, setups, takes, the director's circles and your suggestions) and `direction_timeline` (the cut as it stands, often empty). If `editorPaused` is true, stop and say so.
2. **Choose takes.** Circled takes first, always. Where a setup has no circle, use your suggestion and say so in the summary. Never circle a take yourself. Unusable takes stay out.
   **Takes in sync are one moment** (`direction_dailies › syncGroups`: two cameras, a camera and a screen recording, a camera and a sound recorder). Put the group's sound take (`soundAssetID`) on V1, or the angle the brief wants seen most, and never lay its angles end to end after it. Bring the other angles in over it with `cutaway` without a `sourceRange` (give `at` and `seconds`): Direction plays the same moment, lined up to the frame, and keeps it lined up as the cut changes. If the angle on V1 isn't the sound take, `syncSound` its clip so the sound take plays under it. A circle on any take in the group means the director wants that moment. **A camera and a screen recording** share the frame with `layout` (`{op: "layout", range, layout: "Showing"}` by the style's name for it, or `{kind, corner?, size?, shape?, screen?}`), never cutaways, transforms and shaders by hand, even where a rule describes a corner camera: Direction finds which take is which, frames the camera on the face in a bubble, frames the screen, and keeps both in sync. Lay out the whole cut with the film's layouts (the job says which, and the style's rules say when each is used); a passage that's only talk to camera can be `you`. Then the moves: `punchIn` over the talk to camera, `emphasisPunch` on the lines that land the point, `slowPush` on long takes, `autoZoom` over the screen, `tightenPauses`; and the style's transitions by name where its rules say.
   **A voice with no picture can carry the story**: a narration, when the takes are sound on their own or the style or brief says the film is narrated. The narration is then the spine. `insert` its takes in script order on A1 (a take with no picture goes there by itself) and cut them by their words like any take (`removeWords`, `tightenPauses`, a breath kept where the style keeps one). The picture goes over it as `cutaway`s with a `sourceRange` and `at`. They play without their own sound, so the narration is all that's heard, and every second of it has picture over it: no black. Change the picture with the sentence, not on a timer: a shot comes in where the words name what it shows and holds through the line. When the narrator comes on camera (a passage of talk to camera, often the second half), `insert` that take on V1 like any talking head, with its own sound and its b-roll over it as cutaways; the narration and the talk read as one transcript. The footage's own sound comes up only where the style or the brief asks for it (`audio` on that cutaway). Music goes on A2 and ducks under A1.
3. **Footage first.** Check what you're unsure of with `direction_contact_sheet`, `direction_search_footage`, `direction_screen_text` and `direction_transcript` before placing it. Don't make anything with a service for an assembly.
4. **Build in script order** with `direction_edit`, scene by scene: `insert` each take on V1 (a narration on A1) with the part of it that plays (`sourceRange`), then shape the joins with `roll` (move a cut point) and `splitEdit` (sound leads or carries over a picture cut) so dialogue plays naturally. Keep every must-keep. Aim for the brief's length; an assembly may run long, never short of a must-keep. One scene per batch, with a clear `summary` and a fresh `idempotencyKey`; if the revision moved, read the timeline again and resend.
5. **Report as you go.** `direction_turnover_update` with `state: cutting`, a short `phase` ("Assembling 3 of 5") and `percent`. Locked scenes refuse recutting (`scene_locked`): leave their cut as it is and say so. Their color and sound stay open.
6. **Watch it.** `direction_watch` every scene you built, start to end. Fix a bad join before you deliver, not after.
7. **Deliver.** `direction_turnover_complete` for the assembly turnover with the final `revision`. It's named Assembly for you. The summary says what's in it in a sentence, with the length: "Five scenes, circled takes throughout, 4:12. Scene 3 uses my suggestion 3B T1; nothing was circled there."

Write like an assistant editor: first person, short, concrete, numbers over adjectives. No apologies.
