---
name: edit-video
description: Cut, trim, reframe, balance, mix, caption, add graphics to, or export footage in a Direction film when the user asks directly, outside a turnover.
---

Resolve the film from the user's request or `direction_projects` and pass its `projectPath` on every call. If the film has an open turnover, follow the `take-turnover` skill instead; for a first cut from circled takes, `assemble`.

1. **Read the film.** `direction_brief` (what it's for, how it should feel, length, must-keeps, avoid, and its style if it has one: read it with `direction_style` and follow it), `direction_dailies` (takes, circles, sync groups) and `direction_timeline` (the cut as OpenTimelineIO plus an outline with every clip's id and the `editRevision`). `direction_edit_catalog` lists every edit function, effect, transition and error code with exact ranges; read it rather than guessing fields.
2. **Find what you need in the footage.** `direction_index` looks at footage once (shots, contact sheets, sounds, text on screen; a job). Then `direction_search_footage` ("the pricing slide", "wide of the desk"), `direction_shots`, `direction_contact_sheet`, `direction_screen_text` and `direction_transcript` (timed words, with ids for word cuts). `direction_sync_takes` links takes that recorded the same moment (two cameras, a camera and a screen recording) by their sound. `direction_transcribe` makes words for a take that has none.
3. **Cut with edit functions.** `direction_edit` is the only way to change the cut: `{baseEditRevision, idempotencyKey, summary, operations: [{op, …}]}`, times in cut seconds, frames at the cut's rate. Send substantial batches with `validateOnly: true` first. The verbs cover story (`insert`, `overwrite`, `removeRange`, `trim`, `roll`, `slip`, `slide`, `splitEdit`, `replaceTake`, `removeWords`, `tightenPauses` for pauses, `speed`, `freeze`), picture (`reframe` with `follow: face`, `balance`, `matchBalance`, `cameraLUT`, effects with keys: `transform`, `pin`, `mask`, `blur`, filters, your own Metal `shader`), layers (`cutaway` for B-roll and picture-in-picture, `placeGraphic`, `placeMade`), transitions (`dissolve`, dips, and custom `shader` transitions in the film's Transitions folder) and sound (`gain`, `fade`, `duck`, `syncSound`, `placeMusic`, `audio` for EQ and compression). The `transitions` skill has recipes for transitions cut from the footage itself. On `stale_revision`, read the timeline again and replan. A failed batch commits nothing; history is kept, and `direction_restore` brings back any revision as a new one.
4. **Graphics and looks.** Graphics are JavaScript Direction renders: follow the `make-graphic` skill. The look is the director's: propose looks with `propose-look`, never apply one.
5. **Watch what you changed.** `direction_watch` gives a filmstrip of a passage with its sound described; `direction_frame` renders one exact frame and `direction_preview` a short video (up to 30 s, a job). Check cut points and anything over the picture before saying it's done.
6. **Export when asked.** `direction_export` with a new path (it never overwrites), preset `h264`, `hevc` or `prores`, and `width`/`height` for another size of the same shape, such as the footage's 3840 × 2160. Exports, previews, transcription and indexing are jobs: poll `direction_job` until they finish.

Footage must be SDR (HDR is refused at import). `direction_measure_audio` reports sample peak and RMS, not LUFS. Say plainly when something isn't supported rather than working around it.
