---
name: edit-video
description: Cut, trim, reframe, balance, mix, caption, add graphics to, or export footage in a Direction film when the user asks directly, outside a turnover.
---

Resolve the film from the user's request or `direction_projects` and pass its `projectPath` on every call. If the film has an open turnover, follow the `take-turnover` skill instead; for a first cut from circled takes, `assemble`.

1. **Read the film.** `direction_brief` (what it's for, how it should feel, length, must-keeps, avoid, and its style if it has one; `direction_style` gives the house rules and the style's rules, layouts and moves to follow), `direction_dailies` (takes, circles, sync groups) and `direction_timeline` (the cut as OpenTimelineIO plus an outline with every clip's id and the `editRevision`). `direction_edit_catalog` lists every edit function, effect, transition and error code with exact ranges; read it rather than guessing fields.
2. **Find what you need in the footage, then the library.** `direction_library` lists the director's own music, sound effects, logos and stills, kept outside any film; `direction_import` with `library` brings one in as the film's copy (never footage). A song kept in parts (stems: drums, bass, instruments, melody, and maybe its full mix) comes in whole: `placeMusic` with `parts` plays some of them, `from` a moment of the song, and the `parts` edit says where each is heard (leave the melody out under talking and bring it in where the voice stops); its parts stay in time with each other on their own. The director's note on the song is its map: where the drums come in, where the big part is. `direction_index` looks at footage once (shots, contact sheets, sounds, text on screen; a job). Then `direction_search_footage` ("the pricing slide", "wide of the desk"), `direction_shots`, `direction_contact_sheet`, `direction_screen_text` and `direction_transcript` (timed words, with ids for word cuts). `direction_sync_takes` links takes that recorded the same moment (two cameras, a camera and a screen recording) by their sound. `direction_transcribe` makes words for a take that has none.
3. **Cut with edit functions.** `direction_edit` is the only way to change the cut: `{baseEditRevision, idempotencyKey, summary, operations: [{op, …}]}`, times in cut seconds, frames at the cut's rate. Send substantial batches with `validateOnly: true` first. The verbs cover story (`insert`, `overwrite`, `removeRange`, `trim`, `roll`, `slip`, `slide`, `splitEdit`, `replaceTake`, `removeWords`, `tightenPauses` for pauses, `speed`, `freeze`), picture (moves Direction works out on the face: `punchIn` on every jump cut, `emphasisPunch` on one line, `slowPush` across long shots, `autoZoom` through a screen recording where the action is and `zoomTo` a point or words on screen, with the style's numbers unless you give your own; `reframe` with `follow: face`, `balance`, `matchBalance`, `cameraLUT`, effects with keys: `transform`, `pin`, `mask`, `blur`, filters, your own Metal `shader`), layers (`cutaway` for B-roll, `layout` for how a camera and a screen recording in sync share the frame, by the style's name for it (`layout: "Showing"`) or as you in a bubble on the screen, you, the screen, side by side, the screen framed on a background; zooms, blurs and freezes on a layout's screen survive a layout change; `placeGraphic`, `placeMade`), transitions and effects from the director's techniques (`direction_techniques`; the `technique` edit by name), transitions (the style's by name, with their sound; `dissolve`, dips, and custom `shader` transitions in the film's Transitions folder) and sound (`gain`, `fade`, `duck`, `syncSound`, `placeMusic`, `parts` for a song's stems, `audio` for EQ and compression). The `transitions` skill has recipes for transitions cut from the footage itself, and the `reframe` skill frames a cut for a shape its footage wasn't shot in (`format` changes the cut's size). On `stale_revision`, read the timeline again and replan. A failed batch commits nothing; history is kept, and `direction_restore` brings back any revision as a new one.
4. **Graphics and looks.** Graphics are JavaScript Direction renders: follow the `make-graphic` skill. The look is the director's: propose looks with `propose-look`, never apply one.
5. **Watch what you changed.** `direction_watch` gives a filmstrip of a passage with its sound described; `direction_frame` renders one exact frame and `direction_preview` a short video (up to 30 s, a job). Check cut points and anything over the picture before saying it's done.
6. **Export when asked.** `direction_export` with a new path (it never overwrites), preset `h264`, `hevc` or `prores`, and `width`/`height` for another size of the same shape, such as the footage's 3840 × 2160. Preset `timeline` writes a folder for Final Cut Pro or DaVinci Resolve (FCPXML, renders, sound by track, the look as LUTs) when the director wants to finish elsewhere. Exports, previews, transcription and indexing are jobs: poll `direction_job` until they finish.

Footage is SDR: HDR comes in as an SDR copy, and a file with open keyframes as a copy that plays from any frame, both in the film's Footage folder. `direction_measure_audio` on a take reports its sample peak and RMS, not LUFS; without `assetID` it measures the cut's mix in LUFS (integrated, ITU-R BS.1770-4) with sample and true peak. Say plainly when something isn't supported rather than working around it.
