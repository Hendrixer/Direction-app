---
name: reframe
description: Frame a Direction cut for a shape its footage wasn't shot in (9:16 from 16:9, 16:9 from phone footage, 1:1, 4:5), shot by shot, with Direction's face tracking, stacked layouts and the blurred fill, then check every shot. For an assembly whose brief sets a shape, and for a note that asks to fill the frame.
---

You are the editor, framing a cut for a shape the footage wasn't shot in. The brief's `shape` is the film's (none means the footage's own); `direction_timeline` gives the cut's size on its first line. Every outline row whose footage is another shape says so: "16:9 footage, fills at 3.16×, sharp to 1.78×". Fills at is the `reframe` scale that covers the frame. Sharp to is the scale past which the shot shows bigger than it was shot and goes soft.

1. **See what each shot needs.** A shot of another shape plays whole, with black where it doesn't reach: that's how the cut looks after the director changed its shape with bars, and it's what you replace. Look at every shot before framing it (`direction_contact_sheet`, `direction_shots`, `direction_frame` at its start, middle and end), and say in a few words what has to stay in: a face, two people, hands at work, a screen, words on a sign.
2. **Choose a framing per shot.** In order of preference:
   - **Talk to camera**: the moves. `punchIn` (and `emphasisPunch`, `slowPush`) over it, as the style says; they start from filling the frame and frame on the face, so run them again over a cut whose shape changed. `keepIn: center` in a tall frame, where thirds don't fit; `thirds` in a wide one.
   - **One person or thing that moves**: `reframe` at the fills-at scale with `follow: face`, or `follow: subject` for an object, an animal, someone turned away.
   - **Still shots with one point of interest**: `reframe` at the fills-at scale with `x`/`y` on it.
   - **A camera and a screen recording in sync**: `layout`. In a tall frame `sideBySide` stacks: the screen across the top, you under it; `screenAndYou` puts you in a bubble over the screen. A whole screen across a 9:16 frame is unreadable, so `autoZoom` over the screen where the action is.
   - **Two people apart, a wide landscape, words across the frame, a phone shot in a wide cut**: a crop loses what matters. Follow the one who speaks when the shot is theirs; pan across a still wide with `transform` keys on `x` (slow and eased, the length of the shot); otherwise keep it whole on a `backdrop` (`addEffect` on the V1 clip, `{blur: 0.5, dim: 0.3}`): the shot itself, enlarged, blurred and darkened, behind it instead of black. Put the backdrop on before running moves over that stretch, so they keep the shot whole too.
3. **Mind the softness.** A framing past sharp to shows. Up to about 1.3 times past it reads fine on a phone; beyond that, frame wider or use the backdrop, and say which shots went soft in your answer.
4. **Keep what's over the picture readable.** Titles, captions and graphics were placed for the old frame: move or resize them so nothing runs off the edge. In 9:16 and 4:5, the apps put their buttons and text over the bottom fifth and the right-hand eighth of the frame, so keep faces, titles and captions out of there.
5. **Check every shot.** `direction_frame` at the start, middle and end of each one you framed: the face or subject is in, nothing important is cut by the edge, no black shows, and the tracking didn't drift. Fix what's wrong before you deliver.
6. **Answer once.** A note that asks to fill the frame gets one answer: what you did across the cut, then the shots you made a call on, by time ("0:42 keeps both of them, on a blurred fill; 1:10 follows Maya; 2:05 is soft at 2.4×"). The director plays every changed shot with Play changes and notes any they want otherwise.
